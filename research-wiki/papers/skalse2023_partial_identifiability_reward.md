# Invariance in Policy Optimisation and Partial Identifiability in Reward Learning

## Why this paper was surfaced

We needed a formal statement of the claim under our "scale anchoring" hypothesis: that training a scoring head **only on orderings** leaves something about it genuinely undetermined, rather than merely under-regularized. This is the paper that proves it, data-source by data-source, and — critically for us — it separates the case where an objective carries *ordinal* information from the case where it carries *cardinal* information.

## One-line thesis

Reward learning from any given data source identifies the reward only up to an equivalence class of invariances; the class is characterized exactly per data source, and *noiseless ordinal comparisons* are the weakest source of all — they pin the reward only up to positive linear rescaling (and leave transitions the data never exercises entirely free).

## Key Results

- **Theorem 3.10 (the load-bearing one for us).** Noiseless ordinal trajectory comparisons determine `R` only **up to positive linear scaling** plus a mask over impossible/unobserved transitions. Order information carries no scale. A head trained purely on "which is better" is by construction unidentified in exactly the dimension our controller needs (units).
- **Theorem 3.8 — noise is information.** Comparisons generated by a *Boltzmann-rational* comparator (choice probability a known logistic function of the return difference) determine `R` up to a much tighter class: the stochasticity supplies **cardinal** information, because the observed choice frequency encodes the magnitude of the gap and not only its sign. Scale becomes identified only when the likelihood ties probability to magnitude through a **known temperature**.
- **Theorem 3.2 / 3.3 — behavioural sources are weaker still.** An optimal policy determines `R` only up to optimality-preserving transformations; a Boltzmann-rational policy up to successor-state redistribution and potential shaping. Behaviour-only supervision (which is what a policy-gradient signal is) is one of the loosest data sources in the taxonomy.
- **The ambiguity is benign for some downstream tasks and not others.** For "compute an optimal policy", most sources' ambiguity classes are harmless. Noiseless ordinal fragment comparisons are singled out as *not* harmless, because zero-preserving monotonic transformations are not optimality-preserving in stochastic environments. Ambiguity is only benign relative to a stated downstream use.
- **Theorem 4.2 — transfer kills it.** Under a change of dynamics, most sources leave the reward essentially unconstrained in the deployment environment.

## Relevance to This Project

**It converts our empirical complaint into a theorem.** Our utility tower is trained by leave-one-out advantages and by a counterfactual pair loss `-ΔU·(q−0.5)` — a *sign-plus-weight* ordinal signal, not a likelihood that ties `q` to `ΔU` through a known temperature. By Theorem 3.10 the resulting scoring function is identified at best up to positive linear scaling, which is precisely why margins `u(a_i)−u(a_j)` carry no physical units, and why "is this margin below the 0.044 measurement floor?" is not a well-posed question about the current head. This is not a tuning defect; the objective does not contain the information.

**It also names the minimal fix and its price.** The paper's own contrast between Theorem 3.10 and Theorem 3.8 says the cheapest route from ordinal to cardinal is not necessarily a separate regression head — it is a likelihood whose *temperature is fixed and known* so that the probability of preferring `a_i` becomes a stated function of the utility gap in real units. Our `-ΔU·(q−0.5)` loss is one step short of that: it weights by measured magnitude but never asks `q` to *equal* anything, so nothing forces the margin scale. Either fix (calibrated likelihood, or explicit regression on measured ΔU) supplies the missing cardinal constraint; the paper is agnostic about which, but emphatic that ordinal-only supervision cannot.

**And it warns against overclaiming the fix.** Theorem 4.2's transfer result says identifiability holds only over the transitions the data actually exercises. Our measured ΔU exists only on the counterfactual pairs we probe; margins on unprobed action pairs stay in the unconstrained mask regardless of anchoring. Anchoring makes probed margins mean something; it does not automatically make *all* margins mean something.

## Design question it bears on

Does the tower need an explicit cardinal (regression or calibrated-likelihood) training signal, or is the existing ΔU-weighted ordinal loss enough? This paper answers: not enough, by construction — the ΔU-weighted ordinal loss lives on the Theorem 3.10 side of the line, and only a magnitude-matching likelihood or regression crosses to the Theorem 3.8 side.

## Caveats

- The theorems are stated for reward functions over MDP transitions with infinite data; our setting is a per-menu action scorer with finite, noisy ΔU probes. The invariance classes transfer as *upper bounds on what could be identified*, not as a guarantee that a regression head will identify it on 63 documents.
- The paper says nothing about ties specifically. Exact reward ties are a degenerate case of its masks (the data never distinguishes the pair), which is consistent with our measurement but not separately analysed.
- Nothing here says an anchored scale is *stable under optimization*; identifiability is an infinite-data statement, drift is an optimization statement.
