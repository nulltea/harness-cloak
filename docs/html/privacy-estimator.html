Privacy EstimatorRelation classification and formal universe count prediction

architecture report · 2026-08-01
Abstract

Privacy estimation for lattice substitutions requires two judgments: whether a candidate preserves the source relation, and how large the candidate's formal anonymity universe is; the current uniform pooled representation may not retain the interactions needed for the first task. The implemented model routes distinct serialized inputs through shared ClinicalMosaic weights, adapts only the top four encoder layers for four-way relation classification, and trains a detached count head over frozen candidate features. After two rounds of relation-label auditing, the relation head reached 0.7028 development macro-F1, effectively unchanged from its predecessor's 0.7039. Health-condition macro-F1 rose by 0.0244 while drug and procedure macro-F1 fell by 0.0328 and 0.0272, and context-bearing input trailed type-matched shuffled context by 0.0065. The count head reduced candidate-macro log-MAE from the 0.8298 mean baseline to 0.6216, a 25.1% reduction, and achieved 0.8625 narrow-to-broad ordering accuracy. These results point to representation and interaction limits in the relation branch rather than another broad label-cleaning pass, but the evidence comes from one seed on development data; test data remains sealed, and neither task metric measures realized privacy.

Model Architecture

two inputs · shared weights · disjoint objectives

The privacy estimator is one ClinicalMosaic encoder used in two separate task pipelines, not a joint forward pass with a shared input.

Relation path. The model serializes runtime type, detected source, candidate substitution, and the marked occurrence excerpt into two tokenizer segments. ClinicalMosaic converts the complete sequence into token states, then one masked mean operation collapses every non-special state into a 768-dimensional vector. A LayerNorm and 128-wide GELU head produces logits for valid generalization, reversal, related but non-substitutable, and unrelated. Relation gradients update the head and encoder layers 8 through 11; embeddings and layers 0 through 7 remain frozen.

Count path. The count input contains only runtime type and candidate substitution. It passes through the relation-adapted encoder, but the encoder is frozen and its masked-mean features are cached before count training. A separate 64-wide GELU head predicts log universe size from formal member-set cardinalities. Smooth-L1 regression learns magnitude, and a logistic pairwise loss enforces narrow-to-broad ordering within complete lattice menus.

Architectural limitation. Both paths reduce the whole encoded sequence to one uniform mean vector before either head acts. The relation branch therefore has no explicit source-to-candidate interaction, field-specific pooling, or occurrence-targeted attention. Its performance plateau after label auditing and weak correct-context advantage make this shared representation bottleneck the next architecture question. The successful detached count head remains a useful baseline rather than evidence that count-specific encoder adaptation is unnecessary.
